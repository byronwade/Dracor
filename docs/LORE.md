# Lore

## The Dracor Race

The Dracor are an old-new people. Their bloodlines carry fragments of ancient dragon memory — not through magic or transformation, but through ancestry. Somewhere in the distant past, mortal and dragon intermingled. The result was not a hybrid species, but a mortal people with something extra sleeping in their veins.

### What Dracor Are Not

- Not dragonborn (no full dragon transformation)
- Not lizard people (fully humanoid in form)
- Not half-dragons (no wings, no tails, no scales covering the body)

### What Dracor Are

Humanoid people who carry the faintest echo of dragon nature. This manifests as subtle, often beautiful traits:

- **Ember eyes** — irises that glow faintly amber or gold in low light
- **Scale markings** — patches of iridescent skin along the jawline, shoulders, or forearms
- **Horn ridges** — subtle raised bone along the brow or temples
- **Claw nails** — slightly pointed, dark fingernails
- **Heat shimmer** — the air around them ripples faintly when under stress or exertion
- **Spectral effects** — during ability use, ghostly dragon imagery flickers around them (wings, jaws, claws)
- **Memory marks** — glowing lines that trace along the skin when channeling dragon memory

Not all Dracor display all traits. Some appear almost entirely human until they channel their memory.

## Dragon Memories

Dragon Memories are fragments of ancient dragon consciousness encoded in Dracor bloodlines. They are not spells or powers in the traditional sense — they are inherited instincts, reflexes, and drives from creatures that existed millennia ago.

### Three Known Types

#### Ember (Fire, Passion, Destruction)

The memory of dragons who burned. Ember carriers tend toward intensity — quick to anger, quick to love, quick to act. Their abilities manifest as fire, heat, and explosive force.

*"The ember does not wait. It ignites."*

#### Stone (Earth, Endurance, Protection)

The memory of dragons who endured. Stone carriers tend toward patience — slow to anger, steady under pressure, immovable when committed. Their abilities manifest as earth, shields, and unyielding force.

*"The stone does not break. It holds."*

#### Storm (Lightning, Speed, Chaos)

The memory of dragons who struck. Storm carriers tend toward restlessness — always moving, always adapting, unpredictable. Their abilities manifest as lightning, wind, and sudden violence.

*"The storm does not warn. It arrives."*

### How Memories Work

- Memories awaken gradually through use and stress
- Each memory shapes personality as much as power
- Players choose one memory at character creation — it defines their ability path
- Memories grow stronger with the character, unlocking new abilities over time
- The memory is not a separate entity — it is part of the character's identity

## Ironvale

A frontier town at the edge of the Ironvale Reach. Once a prosperous waystation on the trade roads, Ironvale spent years in isolation after the roads closed. Bandit activity, wild creatures, and the collapse of neighboring settlements cut it off from the wider world.

Now the roads are reopening. New arrivals — Waybound travelers — are helping reconnect Ironvale to civilization. The town is rebuilding, cautiously optimistic, and in desperate need of capable hands.

### Key Features

- **Population:** Small but growing. Permanent residents plus a rotating cast of Waybound travelers
- **Economy:** Contracts fuel the local economy. Waybound take jobs, spend coin at local vendors
- **Governance:** Town council led by practical people. Mara Vale handles the contract board. Decisions made by consensus
- **Architecture:** Stone and timber buildings, some damaged and repaired. A town hall, forge, healer's hut, tavern, and growing residential district

## The Ironvale Reach

A region of forested hills, ancient roads, and abandoned mines. The Reach was once densely settled, but decades of isolation left much of it wild again.

### Zones

- **Ironvale** — the town itself, the social heart of the region
- **The Old Road** — the trade route that once connected Ironvale to the wider world. Now partially cleared, still dangerous
- **Wolfpine Woods** — dense forest north of town. Rich in resources but home to territorial wildlife
- **Ashroot Mine** — an abandoned iron mine east of town. Collapsed in places, rumored to go deeper than anyone remembers

### Atmosphere

The Reach feels like a place waking up after a long sleep. Roads are overgrown but passable. Buildings stand empty but intact. The wilderness reclaimed territory, and now people are taking it back — carefully, one contract at a time.

## Waybound

Waybound is the term for travelers who walk the reopened roads. They take contracts, reconnect isolated towns, clear dangerous routes, and help civilization recover from years of fragmentation.

Players are Waybound. This is not a title given by authority — it is a description of what they do. Anyone who walks the roads and does the work is Waybound.

### What It Means

- **Purpose:** Waybound exist because the world needs them. Roads don't clear themselves. Towns don't reconnect without someone brave enough to walk between them
- **Reputation:** Being Waybound earns respect — slowly. Trust is built through deeds, not words
- **Community:** Waybound tend to gather where there's work. Ironvale is one such gathering point
- **Identity:** Not a guild, not an order, not a faction. Just a word for people who do what needs doing

## Why the World Fears the Dracor

Ancient dragons nearly destroyed civilization. The Sundering — as scholars call it — leveled cities, burned kingdoms, and drove humanity to the edge of extinction. The dragons were eventually defeated, but not forgotten.

The Dracor carry dragon memory. Most people know this. Some fear it.

### The Fear

- "What if the memory awakens fully?"
- "What if they become what their ancestors were?"
- "What if the fire returns?"

### The Reality

- Dracor have existed for centuries without incident
- Their memories grant abilities but do not transform them into dragons
- Most Dracor live ordinary lives
- But the fear persists — especially in isolated communities with long memories

### How Dracor Earn Trust

Towns tolerate Dracor who prove useful. Waybound work is the fastest path to acceptance. Complete contracts. Defend the town. Be visibly, undeniably helpful. Trust is earned through deeds — and displayed on the deed board for all to see.

This tension — being needed but not quite trusted — is central to the player experience. You are powerful and helpful, but the world watches you carefully.
