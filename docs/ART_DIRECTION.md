# Art Direction

## Identity

Dark fantasy frontier. Not high fantasy with gleaming towers. Not grimdark with skull piles. Dracor occupies the space between — a world that was once greater, broke, and is slowly being rebuilt by stubborn people who refuse to let the wilderness win.

## Ember Magic

Magic in Dracor is warm. It glows. The dominant magical aesthetic is ember — orange light, deep reds, flickering particles that trail behind ability use.

- **Ember glow:** Warm orange-amber light emanating from memory marks, shrines, and active abilities
- **Particle trails:** Small floating embers that drift upward during channeling or memory activation
- **Spectral echoes:** Ghostly dragon silhouettes that flicker during powerful ability use — wings, jaws, claws rendered as translucent orange light
- **Memory marks:** Glowing lines that trace along the character's skin when channeling. Subtle at low power, bright at full channel

Ember magic is never neon. Never electric blue or hot pink. It is fire-warm, deep, and grounded.

## Dragon Memory Aesthetics

Each memory type has a distinct visual language:

| Memory | Color | Particle | Surface Effect |
|--------|-------|----------|---------------|
| Ember | Orange, deep red, gold | Floating embers, heat shimmer | Cracks of light in skin, like cooling lava |
| Stone | Earth brown, moss green, slate gray | Dust motes, pebble fragments | Stone texture spreading from hands/feet |
| Storm | Electric blue, white, pale violet | Sparks, arcing lines | Crackling energy along veins and weapon |

These effects are reserved for ability use and memory activation. During normal gameplay, characters appear human with subtle draconic features.

## Environment Palette

| Element | Colors |
|---------|--------|
| Earth and terrain | Deep browns, clay reds, dark soil |
| Stone and cliffs | Slate gray, weathered granite, moss-stained |
| Trees | Dark pine green, faded autumn golds |
| Fog and atmosphere | Cool blue-gray, lavender at dawn, warm amber at dusk |
| Sky | Overcast gray with breaks of warm light, deep blue-black at night |
| Ember highlights | Orange, amber, gold — used sparingly for magical elements |
| Water | Dark teal, reflecting sky color, white foam |

The world is dominated by cool, muted tones. Warm ember colors are reserved for magic, fire, and significance — making them stand out against the environment.

## Architecture

Ironvale and its surrounding structures follow frontier practicality:

- **Materials:** Rough-cut stone foundations, weathered timber frames, slate or thatch roofs
- **Style:** Sturdy and functional. Buildings are meant to survive winters and siege, not impress visitors
- **Damage:** Many structures show repair — patched walls, replaced beams, new stone against old. The town was abandoned and is being rebuilt
- **Scale:** Human-scale buildings. No towering spires. The tallest structure is the town hall, at three stories
- **Ancient ruins:** Pre-collapse structures use smoother stone, geometric precision, and occasional dragon motifs. They contrast sharply with the rough frontier style

## Nature

The Ironvale Reach is defined by dense forest and rocky terrain:

- **Pine forests:** Tall, dark-needled trees with thick trunks. Ground cover of fallen needles, ferns, and moss
- **Mossy rocks:** Boulders covered in thick green moss, scattered through forests and along roads
- **Misty valleys:** Low-lying fog that fills valleys in the morning and evening. Visibility drops. Sound muffles
- **Broken roads:** Ancient stone roads, cracked and overgrown but still recognizable. Hints of the civilization that built them
- **Mountain backdrop:** Distant peaks framing the horizon. Snow-capped, unreachable in the current build, but establishing the world's scale

## Lighting

Lighting defines mood more than any other visual element:

- **Default time of day:** Late afternoon, golden hour. Low-angle sun casting long shadows through trees
- **Dusk palette:** The transition from warm gold to cool blue. Most atmospheric moment
- **Firelight:** Warm orange glow from campfires, torches, and forge. Creates pools of warmth against cold fog
- **Interior lighting:** Dim amber from candles and hearths. Shadows are deep. Corners are dark
- **Shrine lighting:** Ember glow radiating outward. The only light source in dark forests that feels intentional

Lighting is never flat. Every scene has a clear light direction, a warm/cool contrast, and visible shadows.

## Silhouette Readability

Every important game element must be identifiable by silhouette alone:

- **Landmarks:** Distinct shapes visible from 200m+ (a twisted dead tree, a stone arch, a tower ruin)
- **Creatures:** Different body proportions per enemy type. A wolf reads differently from a bandit from a drake
- **Players:** Character silhouette distinguishes weapon type (blade users have a visible weapon at hip, bow users on back, staff users in hand)
- **Shrines:** Tall, narrow structures with a bright emissive element at top. Visible through fog

If a player squints, they should still know what they're looking at.

## What Dracor is NOT

- **Not generic mobile fantasy:** No oversaturated colors, no chibi proportions, no particle vomit
- **Not neon MMO:** No glowing gear sets that look like Christmas trees, no UI-heavy HUD clutter
- **Not anime-proportioned:** Characters have realistic human proportions with subtle draconic features
- **Not photorealistic uncanny valley:** Stylization keeps characters and environments out of the uncanny valley. Textures have visible brushwork quality, not photoscanned noise
- **Not Fortnite/Overwatch colorful:** The palette is muted and grounded. Color is earned through magic and significance

## Reference Touchstones

These are conceptual references for mood and quality, not style guides to copy:

- **Dark Souls:** Atmosphere, weight, environmental storytelling, ruins that tell a story
- **Skyrim:** Frontier wilderness, the feeling of walking into unknown territory, scale of landscape
- **Dragon Age: Inquisition:** Environment art quality, the mix of nature and ruins, lighting
- **Shadow of the Colossus:** Scale of landmarks, the feeling of being small in a large world

## Character Style

- **Humanoid base:** Realistic human proportions. Athletic but not exaggerated
- **Subtle draconic features:** Ember eyes, scale patches, horn ridges — visible but not dominant
- **Gear style:** Practical frontier equipment. Leather, iron, fur, canvas. Not ornate plate armor
- **Silhouette clarity:** Weapon type readable at distance. Body type distinguishable from NPCs and enemies
- **Memory effects:** When channeling, draconic features intensify — eyes glow brighter, scale marks illuminate, spectral dragon forms flicker

## UI Aesthetic

- **Minimal:** As little on screen as possible. Health bar, minimap, and interaction prompts. Everything else hidden until needed
- **Dark base:** UI elements use the stone-900/950 palette with subtle borders
- **Ember accents:** Interactive elements, highlights, and important information use the orange/amber gradient
- **Transparency:** Panels use backdrop blur and semi-transparency so the world shows through
- **Recedes when not needed:** UI fades when not interacted with. The world is the star, not the interface
